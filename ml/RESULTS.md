# Neural vs n-gram name models — results

Generated by `npm run ml` (`ml/experiment.ts`). Fixed seeds: re-running gives the same numbers.

## Setup
- **Data:** every word list in the app (1043 unique lowercase words after cleaning) split once, at random with a fixed seed: **834 train / 104 validation / 105 test**. No word appears in more than one split.
- **Baseline:** interpolated n-gram (letter pairs → next letter, mixed with shorter contexts, add-k smoothing). Best on validation: weights 0.5 / 0.4 / 0.1, k = 0.01.
- **Neural:** MLP language model written from scratch (letter embeddings → tanh layer → softmax), hand-written backpropagation verified by a gradient check in `src/ml/charlm.test.ts`. 12,000 mini-batch steps per setting.
- **Metric:** perplexity per character on unseen words (lower is better; 27 = random guessing).

## Test set (used once)
| Model | Test perplexity | Without near-duplicates (81 words) |
|---|---|---|
| n-gram baseline | **12.497** | 13.266 |
| Neural MLP | **14.276** | 15.339 |

Difference (n-gram − neural): **-1.779**, 95% paired-bootstrap interval **[-3.127, -0.533]** over 105 test words.
**Verdict:** the n-gram model is better, and the interval excludes 0.

- **Training-seed variance:** the chosen neural setting retrained with 4 seeds gives test perplexity **14.42 ± 0.40** (mean ± SD; range 14.14–15.00). The bootstrap above only covers which test words were drawn, so this is the other half of the uncertainty.
- **Near-duplicates:** 24 of 105 test words are one edit away from a training word (e.g. *nebula*/*nebular*). Both models score better on those, so the column without them is the fairer absolute number; the comparison between models is similar.

## Interpretation (generated from the numbers above)
- **Prediction:** the n-gram model predicts unseen words better.
- **Generation:** the neural model produces more valid names (92.2% vs 47.5%, a statistically clear difference). **About 96% of that gap comes from word length**: the n-gram stops too early or runs on far more often (49.3% vs 6.3% rejected for length). The average length is similar, but the n-gram's lengths are much more spread out: SD 3.7 vs 1.8, against 1.4 for real words. Sound-based rejections differ too, but they are a small part of the gap (3.2% vs 1.5%). So the main measured advantage is that the neural model **ends words at more realistic lengths** — its 4-letter context sees further back than the n-gram's 2 when deciding to stop. Why exactly isn't tested here.
- **Overfitting:** bigger networks fit the training words far better than unseen ones (gap up to 10.8 perplexity points; the largest setting reached train 5.06 but validation 15.25). Stronger weight decay gave the better validation score at every size where both were tried; the best setting on validation was 64 hidden units with decay 0.001. More data, not a bigger model, is the fix.
- **Baseline, precisely:** this is a smoothed n-gram sampled *without* filters. The app's own Markov generator (`src/core/markov.ts`) is unsmoothed and **rejects** short, long, unpronounceable and blocked words before showing them, so in the app every shown invented word is already valid — the gap above shows how many tries each model needs, not what users see.

## Failure analysis
- Rejected samples, n-gram: `e`, `mo`, `shnkiell`, `ci`, `nus`, `javspr`, `ami`, `oh`
- Rejected samples, neural: `iva`, `nom`, `srbliat`, `veapeshinno`, `silchrox`, `hestrcxe`, `nib`, `sil`
- Hardest test words, n-gram: outlaw, viraj, nexus, app, glitch
- Hardest test words, neural: app, fox, pop, ruby, sketch

## Limitations
- **Small data:** 1043 words, hand-written, mixing English words, Hindi words and Indian and international names. Neural models usually need far more data to clearly beat n-grams, so this is a fair small-data comparison, not a general claim.
- **Perplexity isn't quality.** A lower score means better prediction of real words, not necessarily names people like; the user study (`docs/user-study.md`) is what measures that.
- **Next step:** a larger, clearly licensed name dataset, then re-run this exact script.
